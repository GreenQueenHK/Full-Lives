Show concept

Full Lives

A life-aspirational interview show: Diary of a CEO for women, with a health and wellness lens.

Where ambitious women talk honestly about building, leading, recovering, and redefining success.

Why now

The white space

Not just business

The show connects the real questions ambitious women are already living: how to build, lead, recover, decide, and evolve.

A global audience fit

The lens is broad enough for women aged 20–50 across markets, but sharp enough to feel premium and specific.

Emotional plus practical

Episodes should feel aspirational and useful, with stories people want to share and lessons people can apply.

Format advantage

Long-form interviews create depth and trust; clips create discovery. Remote-first production keeps costs low and access high.

Audience

Who this show is for

Core demographic

Women and womyn roughly 30 to 50, English-speaking, college educated, globally minded.

Economically active

Working professionals with jobs, income, spending power, and decision-making authority.

Modern healthcare mentality

Open to prevention, diagnostics, supplements, fitness, therapy, data, and evidence-backed wellness.

Family health gatekeeper

Often responsible for the health decisions of partners, kids, and aging parents — the sandwich generation effect.

Socially connected

Active on social media, discovery-driven, and likely to share ideas, brands, and conversations that feel useful.

Identity fit

Ambitious but not one-dimensional — interested in health, work, money, relationships, purpose, and becoming.

Media gap

What is missing in media for women

Beauty magazines

Too superficial; they often treat women’s health as appearance, trends, and product shopping.

Fitness media

Too body-obsessed; the framing often centers weight, aesthetics, and discipline rather than strength and healthspan.

Wellness platforms

Too woo-woo; they can drift into vague, elite, or non-evidence-based advice that leaves thoughtful women unconvinced.

Scientific papers

Too technical; the research exists, but it is not packaged for intelligent lay audiences who want context and actionability.

Femtech communities

Too investor-focused; they speak to capital and founders more than to women trying to navigate their own lives and health.

Health guru podcasts

Too male-centric; many influential long-form health shows are framed through male bodies, male priorities, and male hosts.
